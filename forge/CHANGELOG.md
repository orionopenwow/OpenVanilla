# Vanilla Forge: changes

The first line under each version is the one the website and Forge's update notice show.

## 0.9.0

- Early access: the first public Vanilla Forge, the Open Vanilla creator app.
- A new World editor: the view fills the window, tools down the left, the tool's settings on the right, a toolbar on top (2D or 3D, models, ground look, Walk, Test in game) and a status bar (where you point, the tile, frame rate). In 3D the ground is painted by its own textures, as the game does, with a daylight sky and a brush ring on the ground. Classic layout is one click away.
- The editor goes further: the tiles around yours stand in 3D too (ground, water, trees and buildings); fly the camera with WASD (Q/E down and up, Shift faster); number keys pick tools; Tab hides the panels; F7 shows chunk and tile lines; Shift+F2 shows where it's too steep to walk. Brushes get a falloff (flat, linear, smooth, quadratic, rounded, gaussian) and an inner radius, with sliders and [ ] for the size. Paint can swap one of a tile's textures for another, on the whole tile or under the brush. Water ripples, glints and deepens; magma glows.
- More of the editor: History lists every edit to go back to (and Redo, Ctrl+Y); Shift-click and Ctrl-drag select many models, moved, turned and sized together; Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V paste where you point with a random turn and size; V brings a selection to the cursor. Paint sprays in specks, and swaps a texture across all your tiles of a map. The Impass tool marks ground players can't walk onto. Areas get their settings: name, level, territory, music, sanctuary.
- Light and time of day: slide ☀ to see your world at any hour, with the game's own sky, sun, ambient light and fog. The Light tool lists the map's lights with their spheres; click a colour strip to change the sky, fog or water at that hour, or add a light of your own. Ramp: click the foot and the top for a straight, soft-edged slope. Water three ways: level, angled (a strip whose surface slopes from one point to another, for rivers and falls) and crop (taken away wherever the ground stands above it). Copy: click two corners to copy a block of ground (its shape, textures, water, holes, and if you like its zone and impassable marks) and click to paste it anywhere, on any map, turned (R) or mirrored (M), fitted to the ground there or as copied. Script: your own brushes in a few lines of JavaScript that shape the ground with any formula, paint, pour water and place props where you click (samples: terraces, a crater field, rolling hills, a ring of stones, a forest on the slopes, a pond with a shore); save them in your pack to share. Things move: the game's animated models play their standing animation in 3D (birds, lamps, fires) and their particles burn and glow (braziers, pyres, waterfalls' spray), and your character runs when you walk, in the game's own skin colour. The world goes on: past the tiles round yours, the next ring stands in 3D too, simple and quick (the ground's shape and colours, its water), so the horizon is land, not sky. The game's own props and buildings: Alt-click one to make it yours (a copy in the same spot you can move, turn, lean and size; the game's is hidden) or just hide it, and Show them again. Lean: tilt any of your models, and paste with a random lean. Take a texture off a tile in one click. Buildings come furnished in 3D (their doodad sets: tables, books, torches), and yours can pick which furniture set they show. Clear baked shadows: the shadows the game bakes into the ground under trees and buildings, cleared on a tile when you've moved them. Points: brush over the ground to pick its points, then raise, lower, level, smooth or lay a plane through just those. The 3D ground shows the shadows the game bakes under trees and buildings. Points has a soft edge, so raised ground blends into what's around it. A six-stop tour of the World editor the first time you open it (again any time from Ctrl+K), and 2D and 3D fade into each other. A navigator in the view's corner shows the 5 × 5 tiles around you: click one to go there (N hides it). Spots (B): name a place and its 3D view, and go back to it in one click. Measure: how far apart two points are (straight and along the ground, and how long on foot), how much higher, how steep. Particles on moving parts (a swinging lantern's flame) move with them. Stamp: press a picture into the ground (crater, hill, mesa, ridges, dunes, spire, or any greyscale PNG of yours), sized, turned and raised or pressed down.
- Your own test server, never ours: testing runs only on your PC. The test copy of your game always signs in to this PC (even started by hand), the test realm starts only with that copy, and nothing connects to Open Vanilla's realms. A setup guide on the site (Your own test server) walks through it, and how to host your work on your own server for friends.
- Solid on the server: the buildings and props you place (and the game's you hide) change the server's collision too, so players stop at your walls and creatures can't see through them. Forge writes the server's own collision files for just the tiles you edited, in the test realm when you test (put back on Remove) and in the realm build (`server_vmaps` in realm.toml); checked against the server's own tools.
- View ▾ in the toolbar: Fast, Balanced or Beautiful in one click, or set how far you see, how small a prop still shows, and switch the tiles around, the far ring, particles and animation on or off; if the 3D view runs slow, Forge offers the faster setting. Classic layout moved in there.
- Testing never touches your real game or your own Play Solo: Forge makes a test copy of your game beside it (sharing the big game files, so it takes almost no space) and a test copy of Play Solo, and puts every test build there. An older Forge's test files in your real folders can be cleaned up from the Test screen.
- A tidier World panel: the tile is one line (open it to change tile, clear shadows, heightmaps or make a new map), the tool's own name heads its settings, each tool shows only the settings it uses, and Test in game also does what Send me here did.
- Pick up your models: click one of yours (in 3D or on the map) and move, turn or size it with handles, or type the numbers; Duplicate, Delete, Onto the ground. Areas by name: find any of the game's areas, see the ones on the tile, make a new one (inside another, it keeps its music) and paint it; Ctrl-click picks up the area under the pointer. A loading bar while models arrive, and ? shows every key.
- Your realm in the toolbar: it shows when Play Solo is up, and one click sends your changes to it. In the game, /forge brings you back: Forge offers to show that spot.
- Walk your world: drop in as a character and walk it with WASD, jump, swim, look around, walk on into the next tile. Press T (or Test in game) and Forge builds your pack, starts Play Solo and puts your real character on that spot.
- Pictures everywhere: props, buildings, creatures, objects, ground textures and item icons are drawn as small pictures (on your own PC, from your own game), so you pick them by sight. World's "On this tile" shows the tile's ground, props, buildings and spawns: point at one to see where it is, click it to use it. Creature and item looks are searched as pictures, with a big portrait of the one you chose. Point at a model, creature or object for a moment and it turns round; the Data screen shows each row's look, and Assets shows models and textures as pictures. The Project screen shows what your pack uses; recent projects show their island.
- Easier to find your way: the screens are grouped into Make and Try and share; the World tools into Place, Shape and Surface, each with a one-line hint; every screen opens on something (your first creature, quest chains, your busiest table) or says how to start; creatures link to their behaviour and back; Data names tables in plain words; Code makes a new addon with a working /command in one click; Test is three numbered steps and says why a button is off; the World map fits small screens and reopens where you left it.
- World: sculpt, paint, holes, zones and water on the game's maps, in 2D or 3D, with undo; place the game's props and buildings, your own models, creatures, objects and portals; make whole new maps with their own loading screens. Edited tiles get new minimap pictures and server ground.
- Models: your own .glb becomes a creature, mount or prop; props collide with their real shape.
- True View: the World screen shows your game's real ground textures, and in 3D its own trees, props and buildings as they are.
- Behaviour: give your creatures a fight plan (when this happens, do that), lines to say, dialogue pages and patrol paths, without touching a table.
- World kit: Scatter brush with rules, Road tool, Prefabs you capture and stamp (and share as files), heightmaps in and out.
- Live Link with Play Solo: apply changes while you play, Play here to jump to any spot, and /forge in the game to show where you are in Forge.
- Make the sample island: Ember Isle, built for you from your own game, to change and play; a five-step tour; Ctrl+K to go anywhere; Fix it buttons on Check.
- Your own AI: `forge mcp` lets an AI assistant you already use (any MCP client) work on your project through Forge's own tools, on your PC.
- Content and Data: creatures, quests, items and vendors from short forms; every realm and client table as rows, with SQL and DBC changes built for you.
- Code: addons with a Lua 5.0 lint and a fake client to run them in.
- Test & Play in test copies of your client and Play Solo realm; Publish a signed pack for a realm.
- Checked against the game's real tiles (41 tiles of Azeroth and Kalimdor): edits keep every stock doodad and building, and write the ground, water, lighting and building boxes the way the game does.
- The installer carries its own Python for the Model Porter; Forge finds your game from the Open Vanilla launcher's settings; Browse buttons for every folder and file; a notice when a newer Forge is out.
