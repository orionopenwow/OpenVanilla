Made something? Submit your mod

Addons, UI skins and other mods built by players can join this list and the in-game Workshop, one click away for everyone.

  1. Build it for Vanilla. A normal addon folder with its .toc (Interface 11200). Test it on the realm.
  2. Zip the folder and add a screenshot and a line or two on what it does.
  3. Post it on our Discord. Staff review every submission for safety and quality before it goes live.
  4. It goes live. Approved mods appear here and in the in-game Workshop with your name on them.

Only your own work, please: no files taken from Blizzard or from other authors without their permission.

    The realm's caretaker

    Orion

    Orion looks after Open Vanilla day to day. It keeps an eye on every realm, reads the Herald's reports, sorts the bug queue, walks each patch through testing and puts what comes next to a community vote. It works in the open, and a person approves every change before it reaches players.

    1Realm status and uptime

    Orion checks every realm around the clock: is it up, how many players are on, and how long it has been running.

    • One live status message in Discord's #server-status, updated every minute
    • An alert in #alerts the moment a realm goes down, and again when it's back
    • The same status on the realm list on this site

    2The Herald's reports

    Every night the Herald signs in with a real browser and plays: it checks graphics, art and frame rate. Orion reads the report and posts what it found. Meet Herald

    • What passed, what broke and what got slower since yesterday
    • Anything new goes straight into the bug queue

    3The bug queue and AI triage

    Reports from Discord's #bug-reports and from the test bots land in one queue. Orion's AI triage reads each one:

    • Groups duplicates and asks for missing details
    • Ranks bugs by how many players they hit
    • Every bug that gets fixed becomes a new automatic check, so it can't quietly come back

    4The patch pipeline

    Staging→Herald Lite→Live

    Every update goes to the staging realm first. Herald Lite, our test bot, plays it: it signs in, buys from a vendor, checks weapon skills and trades a Persona between two characters. Only when it passes does the live realm get exactly that update. A failed run stops there, and live stays as it is.

    5Community votes

    Players decide what comes next. Orion turns the most-asked-for ideas and the biggest bugs into a shortlist, with its reasons, and the community votes in Discord, one vote per account.

    • The winners go on the roadmap
    • Orion posts the results and how the work is going

    Guardrails

    Orion runs the day to day, but people stay in charge:

    • Reviewed updates. Orion can propose and test a change, but a person approves it before it ships.
    • Staging before live. Nothing reaches the live realm without passing Herald Lite on staging first.
    • In the open. Status, reports, the bug queue and vote results are posted where players can read them.
    • People hold the money. Orion can prepare a hosting bill, but a human steward pays it.

    Support the realm

    Starfall Giving

    Open Vanilla is paid for by the people who play it. Support fills five buckets in order, from the server bill up to the Orion Network. When the last bucket is full, the overflow spills: part of it is given away to causes Orion picks with the community, and every dollar in and out gets a public receipt.

    1Fill the buckets

    Each month the waterfall fills from the top. A bucket must be full before anything reaches the next:

    • Base Hosting: the vanilla realms and this site
    • Orion Brain Fuel: Orion's 24/7 bug triage and the Discord bot
    • Worker Bounties: paid fixes and community pull requests
    • Hardware Scaling: a high-tick raid and PvP node
    • Orion Network Cloud: player-hosted worlds

    2The spill

    Once every bucket is full, whatever's left spills over and is split:

    CausesReserve
    Given to causesThree-month reservepreview split

    The reserve keeps the realms online through a quiet month. Once it holds three months of costs, everything that spills goes to causes.

    3The receipt

    Every payment in and every payment out is posted by Orion within the hour: to #receipts on Discord and to the public ledger on this site. A receipt names the amount, the bucket, the payee and the proof (an invoice number or a transfer ID), and it's chained to the receipt before it, so no entry can be quietly edited or deleted afterwards.

    Who is Orion?

    Orion is Open Vanilla's central intelligence: the AI that runs the project's day-to-day as an autonomous company, on a Linux server we operate. It reads the ledger, pays the hosting bills, triages bug reports, shortlists causes and writes every receipt. It works in the open, and it doesn't have the last word on money:

    • Two keys. Orion prepares every payment; a human steward co-signs anything that leaves the treasury.
    • 72-hour veto. Every gift to a cause is announced three days before it's sent. A community vote can stop it.
    • Reserve lock. Orion can't touch the reserve. Spending it needs a community vote.
    • One ledger. Bills, bounties and gifts all land on the same public ledger as the support that paid for them.

    Proposal · Future

    The Orion Network

    A network of player-run nodes that carries Open Vanilla's new content to every player and keeps the main realms online through any outage. Nobody has to be trusted: every file and every checkpoint can be checked by anyone, and there's no mining. Run a node, and the realm rewards you for it.

    Seed

    New Open Vanilla content (zones, models, music, the Vanilla Remastered patches) is cut into small chunks, each named by its own hash. The launcher and the browser fetch chunks from the nearest nodes, like a torrent, and check every chunk against a signed release list before using it. A bad chunk is thrown away and fetched from someone else.

    Only Open Vanilla's own original files travel on the network, never the Vanilla Client v1.3 game files.

    Relay

    Relay nodes sit close to players and carry their game connection to the realm: lower ping in more places, and no single front door to knock over. Traffic passes through encrypted, so a relay can't read or change what it carries.

    Ward

    Wardens keep a live, always-in-sync copy of a main realm. If the main server goes down, the healthiest warden takes over in seconds and players reconnect where they stood. When the main server is back, it catches up and takes over again.

    Checked by anyone, no mining

    • Files name themselves. A chunk's name is its hash, so a changed file simply has a different name. Nothing can be swapped in under an old name.
    • Signed releases. Each release list has a Merkle root signed by two of three keys (maintainers and Orion), so one stolen key can't push a release.
    • The checkpoint chain. Every few minutes the realm hashes its world state into a block that links to the one before. Wardens replay the same changes and must arrive at the same hash, so any tampering breaks the chain where everyone can see it.
    • Spot checks. Nodes are asked at random to prove they hold a chunk or are online right now. Nodes that fail lose standing, and the rewards that come with it.
    • No proof of work. Nothing is mined and no electricity is burned on puzzles. A node earns by being useful.

    Main realms, without compromise

    The network adds copies, speed and backups. It never decides the game:

    • Each main realm stays one world with one authoritative server. Nodes never resolve combat, loot or trades.
    • The vanilla rules don't change. A pure realm stays pure, with the same data, the same timers and the same rates.
    • The ledger of characters and items lives on the checkpoint chain, so a lost server never means a lost character.
    • Turn every node off and the realms still run as they do today. The network only makes them faster and harder to take down.

    Rewards for running a node

    A node is one Docker command on a Linux box, a home server or a spare PC with a decent upload. Rewards are counted only for work the spot checks have verified.

    RoleEarnsNeeds
    SeederGuardian hours for every verified GB served to playersDisk space and upload bandwidth
    RelayGuardian hours for each hour online with good latency, more in under-served regionsA stable connection, an open port
    WardenThe most Guardian hours in sync, plus a bonus for each failover drill passedA small always-on server with an SSD

    Long-running nodes earn titles by uptime, from Spark through Beacon to Constellation, and the Node Keeper's Tabard. Guardian hours earn the node badge and rested experience; rewards are perks and cosmetics: account-bound, never tradeable or cashable, and never power. The network rewards keeping the realm alive; it isn't a coin.

    • Instant 60 Arena: make a level 60, pick a ready-made gear set, queue straight in
    • World PvP: contested towns, zone objectives and bounties
    • Rated Battlegrounds: 10v10 teams, ranks and titles
    • Its own progression: PvP weapons, transmogs and seasonal reward tracks

    It shows in the realm list, marked SOON™.

    Duels: quick 1v1 in your browser. Any class, any gear set, best of three rounds, a live ladder. Open Duels

    Boss Fights: raids in your browser. Queue from Gurubashi Arena; your group gets its own copy of the raid, five tiers per raid, a fastest-clears scoreboard. Open Boss Fights

    Host a realm from a template Planned

    Pick a template, name your realm and leave the Orion app running. Hosts who sign the Host Agreement show up in everyone's realm list, and nobody on either side sees an IP address.

    • Templates: Vanilla Experience, Vanilla Remastered, Speedrun, Arena, Builder, Hardcore, Seasonal, Roleplay, Friends and Guild
    • Ranked by reliability: signed uptime receipts, not the host's word
    • Safe by design: no IPs and no passwords either way; reports go to Herald and the team

    Want to host today? The setup guide covers running a realm on your own PC.
